# A Mark

2026.01.18

*Written in Chinese by River. Translated into English by ChatGPT.*

My essays begin with an intention—to leave a mark on the world—a mark that says “I am here”. In the book published in Chinese as *On the Side of Humanity*, Czesław Miłosz writes of language trying, and failing, to convey “I am here”.<sup id="note-ref-1"><a href="#note-1">1</a></sup> I am entering into a “struggle” with this problem. It means something to me akin to what the great marlin means to the old man.<sup id="note-ref-2"><a href="#note-2">2</a></sup>

When pain rises like lake water and submerges me, the thought that holds me is—this is my lot. Of all the forms of human courage, “acceptance” seems the hardest. When I “accept” my circumstances, I find a moment's peace.

Body and soul sink together to the bottom. Sound disappears. The world falls silent. I find solace in that silence—rather than in imagining what the future promises.

In the *Diamond Sutra*, Subhuti asks the Buddha: “How should we abide? How should we subdue the mind?” Elsewhere, the sutra says: “Let the mind arise without dwelling on anything.”<sup id="note-ref-3"><a href="#note-3">3</a></sup> “Let the mind arise without dwelling on anything”—how hard that is to do.

My family's love, the affection of a few people—these still seem insufficient to save me, to keep me alive.

When I try to put “I am here” into words, in that instant of “Sisyphean” ascent, I find redemption—that is me straining with all my strength to hold on to my soul. It has almost fallen into the abyss.<sup id="note-ref-4"><a href="#note-4">4</a></sup>
