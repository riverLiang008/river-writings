# A Day of Falling Asleep in the Toilets

2026.01.09

*Written in Chinese by River. Translated into English by ChatGPT.*

For half a year, I have felt as though I were suffering from a long illness. I have not recovered, but I am now willing to let someone push my “wheelchair”, take me out for a while, let me breathe the fresh air beyond the ward. Perhaps that person is my colleague F. Without him, I cannot hold on. I cannot make it through these days that are so hard to bear.

A week ago, I left home and returned to this place that confines me. I close my eyes and recall the train journey—how dejected I felt then. I wrote on my phone:

> “Live by intuition, not by inertia.
>
> 2026 is a year full of hope for me. More precisely, I hope it will be a year full of hope. For no other reason than that time is moving. As long as time moves, I will hold on to hope—like Andy in Shawshank, telling Red about the little seaside town in Mexico he longs to live in. We will meet again. We will embrace—on a sunlit morning, our lives will shine from then on.”

Now a week has passed.

I rarely dream. This time, something felt strange. I dreamed of someone I had never known, a girl, I vaguely remember. I dreamed she was keeping a diary, and I was in it. She wrote: “But she's different. I remembered her so vividly from the first time I saw her. I've never seen anyone so alive—facing the world in this way.”

Every voice tells me: hold on, grit your teeth and keep going. After Chinese New Year in 2026, perhaps there will be a dawn. I cling to this “dawn”. Through it, I stay alive, day after day.

On the afternoon of 9 January 2026, my head felt heavy and foggy. After the lunch break, I hid in the toilets, lowered the toilet lid, covered my eyes with my hat and slept for another twenty minutes.

That evening, I returned to where I was living. After dinner, I fell asleep on the sofa. Woke up. Eleven o'clock. Wrote these words.<sup id="note-ref-1"><a href="#note-1">1</a></sup>
