# The Coin Has Been Tossed, but Hasn't Landed Yet

2026.02.01

*Written in Chinese by River. Translated into English by ChatGPT.*

When it comes to the “predictability” of the future, there are two ways of living through time.

Tomorrow is predictable, and so is the day after. Go on like this long enough, and a person becomes like a piece of rusted iron lying on the ground.

Perhaps many people feel that a night's sleep is all it takes to reach “tomorrow”. But for me, right now, tomorrow is not so easily reached.

At half past seven on the evening of 29 January, I meant only to take a short nap on the sofa. When I opened my eyes again, it was already 6:48 the next morning. Sleep really is a good way to pass the time, carrying us effortlessly into “tomorrow”.

Through the long days of waiting for news,<sup id="note-ref-1"><a href="#note-1">1</a></sup> my consciousness cut my time into tiny fragments.

On the afternoon of 31 January, I was operating software in a stifling laboratory, dressed in a cleanroom suit. In front of me stood several enormous pieces of heavy machinery, metres high. Their installation was not yet complete: steel wires and cables lay exposed inside their gleaming metal shells. During the long wait for the software to run, it suddenly occurred to me that the wind of karma had blown me here, and would one day carry me away. Amid all those tangled threads of causes and conditions, our fate is like a beam of light distorted in space—we think it is bent and folded, yet it remains straight all along, until we reach death.

Every operation in the laboratory is governed by extremely strict procedures. Wrapped in white cleanroom suits, people are reduced from “human beings” to individual components. Within industrial civilisation, rules form a tightly integrated whole; nature is quite the opposite. Only in nature can life spring up like mushrooms after rain. To me, vitality and a sense of the spiritual are forms of possibility. Probably no one would imagine that, in the pauses while I wait for the program to run, I silently recite the *Heart Sutra*. Only then can my sense of time be reawakened—time is a real force, not a conjuring trick.

Sleep can take us easily into “tomorrow”, but it cannot take us into “the future”. We pray, yet still we must lift the weight, shoulder it and walk on.

Now I have reached the afternoon of 1 February. At last I have a little time to myself. In that time, I deftly pack “meaning” into the hours—in my usual way—by writing.

I may leave very soon, or I may not. The coin has been tossed, but hasn't landed yet. I am listening intently, waiting for a sharp clink.<sup id="note-ref-2"><a href="#note-2">2</a></sup>
