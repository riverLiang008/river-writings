# Perhaps I'll Be an Unemployed Drifter Someday

2026.02.08

*Written in Chinese by River. Translated into English by ChatGPT.*

The day I handed in my notice, I was unmoved by my supervisor's advice about my “future prospects”. In fact, I was quite happy with things as they stood—given my temperament, the fact that I hadn't become a wandering artist and actually had a job that paid enough to support me was already cause for universal celebration.

In Liu Zichao's *Arriving Before Midnight*, I came across a passage about Joyce:<sup id="note-ref-2"><a href="#note-2">2</a></sup>

> “Joyce lived in Trieste for nearly ten years, and his writing went quite well. After the First World War broke out, he took refuge in Rome for a while. He wrote almost nothing during that time; only after returning to Trieste did he begin writing again. He gave up his teaching post and became a private tutor, settling into a life of ‘carefree mornings, busy afternoons and chaotic evenings’. He woke at ten every day and lay in bed ‘lost in thought’. At around eleven, he got up, shaved, then sat down at the piano he had bought on instalments. His playing was often interrupted by creditors at the door.
>
> His family asked him what to do.
>
> ‘Let them in,’ he would say, then try to steer the conversation away from debts and towards music or politics.”

I smiled in recognition.

What my circumstances will be a few years from now, I have no idea—whether I'll keep working or become an unemployed drifter, I haven't quite decided. I have little interest in the whole ‘success narrative’ that civilisation has devised. I have a natural aversion to dullness.

But I also understand that “life has its unbearable side wherever you are, whether you choose to be Cain, settled in one place, or Abel, a nomad.” (*Arriving Before Midnight*)<sup id="note-ref-2"><a href="#note-2">2</a></sup> Living is an art of changing things, and an art of enduring them. Over the past six months—or, more precisely, the past three—I burned through my passion, pain and strength. Amid the heat and flames, a door opened, and I stumbled headlong through it. What will my next task be?<sup id="note-ref-1"><a href="#note-1">1</a></sup>
