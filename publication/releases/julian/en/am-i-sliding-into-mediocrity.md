# Am I Sliding into Mediocrity?

2025.08.13

*Written in Chinese by River. Translated into English by ChatGPT.*

Am I sliding into mediocrity?

Am I sliding into mediocrity—something I once hated and feared so deeply?

Living in a glittering big city, studying abroad, then becoming a white-collar worker and travelling to different countries: these were my childhood dreams. Now I have fulfilled them.

How do I feel? I ought to feel content. I ought to feel content. But what am I to do with my passion?

On the one hand, I am content: I have a little place of my own, a space where no one disturbs me, and an income that is stable for now—and quite decent for someone my age. On the other, I doubt myself. I cannot live like this forever, leading a life of utter boredom like the older cousin who works as a photographer in *Uzak*,<sup id="note-ref-1"><a href="#note-1">1</a></sup> slipping into weakness and self-absorption. But what else can I do?

Travel cannot provide an answer that lasts forever. You cannot live forever among things that have nothing to do with you. Yes, that is precisely the beauty of travel: none of it has anything to do with me. And because it has nothing to do with me, I can remain carefree, free to come and go.

I think of the Swiss man we met while trekking in Nepal, the one who gave us stones. He had lived in Thailand for ten years, making his living from Muay Thai. What had drawn him to spend so many years in a culture so different from his own? In his eyes there was loneliness, warmth, sorrow, a sense of having made peace with things. Those contradictory qualities made this man, whom I may have met for the first and only time in my life, unforgettable to me. Had he found his answer?

On the afternoon of 12 August 2025, I saw a beautiful sunset as I rode home. In that moment, I felt a warmth.

Recently I read a book whose Chinese title means *Dying at Thirty*. The title drew me in: I wondered how someone else might face the crisis of losing their passion for life. Yet the story ended with the author meeting a beautiful young woman who led him to open his heart.

Must the answer really be sought in another person? I am puzzled.<sup id="note-ref-2"><a href="#note-2">2</a></sup>
