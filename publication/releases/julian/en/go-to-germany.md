# “Go to Germany”

2026.01.12

*Written in Chinese by River. Translated into English by ChatGPT.*

Questions that are hardly new: what kind of life do I really want? Where do I really want to go?

A few predictable versions:

A. Work hard in a big city. Get promoted. Become a junior manager earning several hundred thousand yuan a year.

B. Marry someone born and raised in a big city. Married life.

C. The YOLO life.<sup id="note-ref-1"><a href="#note-1">1</a></sup> Travel to different countries. Unmarried. No savings.

These “models”, each readily found in someone I “know”, seem to have their own ways of being “successful”. But none seems to be what I want. All seem to be what I fear.

Ever since the thought of going away to work in Germany came to me yesterday, I have been unable to stop thinking about this. Yes—there is no utopia anywhere in the world. Difficulties follow us like shadows. Perhaps I could spend years in Germany and still end up with nothing. Then the question arises—behind the narrative of “escape”, what am I really seeking? How do I want to spend my time? This is not an abstract question. It means: where do I choose to live, how do I spend each day, and how do I face real difficulties?

In *The Baron in the Trees*, Calvino devised a complete path towards freedom; in *Siddhartha*, Hesse devised a path towards “wholeness”. But these are abstractions, fables, the outlines of a way of life that humanity has yet to realise.

So I have to ask myself: what kind of life do you really want?

The answer seems not to lie in any place in the world, but on this path towards myself. Sometimes I can see it; sometimes it disappears from view.

I have decided to start looking for work in Germany. That is the answer. It exists now, and it will exist when I look back long afterwards. If I never go to Germany, the thought will be like a little splash that barely disturbs the surface. The lake grows still again.

My answer is both abstract and clear—I want to pack meaning into my time—as though filling a bookshelf, one book at a time. Yet the realities of society cut my time into countless fragments. All I can do is be a scavenger—pick up a very few of them, polish them into shapes I love, and set them on the windowsill. In that moment, I understood the metaphor of Andy placing the stones he had polished on the windowsill in *The Shawshank Redemption*.

I do not blame “reality”, because it is part of me. It is just that, when I am exhausted and cannot break free however much I want to, I curse it too.

Curse it. Then throw myself back into it. The future? The future does not exist. There is only an image that rises in my mind and lingers there: “go to Germany to work”. It may come true, or it may not.<sup id="note-ref-2"><a href="#note-2">2</a></sup>
