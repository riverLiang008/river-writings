# Afternoon No. 01

> Date: May 2026\
> Location: Shenzhen\
> Conversation partner: A

*Written in Chinese by River. Translated into English by ChatGPT.*

---

She wore a grey knitted top with a folded neckline, and jeans. She had a straight fringe. There was a little design flair to the simple outfit. Tall, slender. Beautiful.

“I'm in a black top and a white skirt,” I messaged her. Just then I noticed another woman by the metro entrance wearing a black top and a white skirt too. “With a backpack,” I quickly added. Seeing A walk towards the other woman, I hurriedly caught her by the arm.

A flicker of surprise crossed her eyes, then she laughed. So did I. “I was just about to tap her on the back. Good thing you stopped me.”

That little mix-up seemed to make us familiar with each other straight away.

As we walked along a tree-lined road, I asked, “I'm curious—what made you decide to travel alone? People usually look for someone to go with. When I was at university, I arranged to go hiking with a friend, but they cancelled on me. So I went on my own. That was my first solo trip.”

“I'd always wanted to travel alone. Then I went to Hainan by myself, and I really enjoyed it,” she said. “But I do want to connect with people in the places I visit. Otherwise it feels as though I haven't really been there. How do you connect with people when you travel?”

“I don't think I deliberately seek it out. Mostly I leave it to chance. Sometimes it's spending a day wandering around a city with a stranger; sometimes it's a brief conversation, or even just a smile and a nod.” I went on, “But I also really enjoy wandering alone somewhere unfamiliar—leaving my usual context behind, all those roles, like employee or someone's child, and just taking things in without any particular aim.”

She nodded.

We came to a gallery showing photographs by amateur photographers: portraits and landscapes.

We looked around for a little while.

“Maybe it's because they're not professionals, but a lot of these feel more like casual snapshots to me. There's quite a distance between this and the work of great photographers,” I said. Perhaps sensing that my judgment was a little sharp, I added, “That's just my opinion, though. Other people might think they're really good.”

She laughed. “Covering yourself, are you? With the way things are online these days, everyone feels they need a disclaimer.”

I laughed too. “Yes. Don't want to get roasted.”

“I agree, though,” she said. “Maybe it's because it's a group exhibition rather than one photographer's work. There isn't such a clear theme or style. Solo photography exhibitions tend to be better.”

“Have you studied photography much?”

She gave a slightly embarrassed smile. “I just like going to exhibitions, trying to develop my eye. I haven't really studied it. There's a lot I don't understand either.”

Leaving the gallery, we walked to a nearby café and sat down.

A ordered one of the signature coffees—I forget its name. I had a yuanyang, a mix of coffee and milk tea.

The café occupied the ground floor of an old residential building. Renovation had turned it into a space full of atmosphere.

We talked about our jobs and internships—a subject that seems hard to avoid at our age. How AI would change work, where to go next, what to do: I wasn't too sure myself.

For me, the difficulty seemed to be not just finding a job, but working out how to live once I had one.

I told her about the absurd things that had happened at my first job. She listened with relish, joining in the complaints whenever a particularly infuriating part came up.

“I'm planning to stay in this job for two years,” she said. “Then I'll quit and take a gap, maybe travel around for a while. After that, I'll look for my next direction in life.” She was about to graduate and start work in a product-related role.

“Yes, exactly. That's what I'm thinking too,” I said.

In the end, we both said, “One step at a time.”

When the conversation turned to relations between women and men, we complained about some men's ridiculous behaviour, and the many ways women were oppressed in a society where men had long held the floor and shaped the narrative.

“Let's focus on our careers first,” I said. Then, laughing, I added, “Not that I have much of a career.”

That made her laugh too. “Not much of a career at all. Ha!”

We parted at the metro station and boarded trains going in opposite directions. Before we said goodbye, we exchanged WeChat details.

If there was a chance, I would probably ask her to hang out again sometime. That was what I was thinking.

Later, I told my mother about the afternoon. She asked something I hadn't expected: “Do you feel under pressure when you're with her?” She meant because A was pretty.

“Well, I can't see myself anyway,” I said.
