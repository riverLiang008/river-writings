# Sayram Lake

*Written in Chinese by River. Translated into English by ChatGPT.*

Right now, I am enjoying lunch at a Dicos beside Sayram Lake.

On social media, I see that someone has photographed rainbow-coloured clouds above the lake. I check the date. Today.

I quickly look out of the window at the sky. But however hard I look, I cannot find the colourful clouds in the photos.

A shame.

I finish the last drop of hot milk tea and throw the cup away. The bin lid lifts and falls shut. I push open the door and step back into the lake's fierce wind.

This morning, I set out from Yining with another small tour group. Each of us kept to a window of the minibus, watching a different view. “That's Sayram Lake beside us,” the driver said. Everyone stirred from their doze, eyes turning towards the windows. The lake, covered in ice and snow, merged with the sky: an endless white. After a while, everyone settled back into their seats, seeming a little disappointed. The driver took us around the lake, stopping at each spot to let us wander on our own.

The cold wind cuts at my face like a knife. I am in the wind, with my shadow. The warmth of the food quickly leaves me. My hands are red with cold, my fingers a little stiff. I switch off the camera and let it hang from its strap around my neck, put on my headphones, start the music and tuck my hands into my pockets.

Unreal.

It is as though I have “suddenly” arrived here—pacing on the ice. Later I will return to the city, and that “I” will “suddenly” have another memory—a memory of beauty, freedom, exile and having nothing to do.

Right now, I have nothing to do.

I watch the people busy taking photos, then quietly turn away.

“Getting ready to leave for the next stop,” says a WeChat message from the guide. So I walk back.

Towards sunset, the snow-covered mountains around us turn pale pink. Where the ice has melted, a few swans float on the lake. I ask a woman carrying a camera to photograph me. Thirty yuan a photo. When she learns that I am choosing only one, she is unhappy. “Look, if you only want one, you need to tell me beforehand. Don't waste everyone's time.” Even as she speaks, she is calling over another customer, busy photographing someone else.

In that photo, there is a faint smile on my face. Black knitted hat, black outdoor jacket, black outdoor trousers. Behind me, Sayram Lake is a jewel-like blue (a blue that can pierce the soul), with a few white swans.

The world is like a long corridor with countless rooms. We choose one, knock, push the door open, and find an entirely new view.

I stand in the cold wind for a long time, looking at this magnificent scene. I think of this morning, when the driver and everyone else had already left the city, leaving only me behind at pick-up—because I had been added to the WeChat group so late the night before, no one had reminded me to send my hotel address.

I think of the mother and daughter on this trip. The daughter sits in the front passenger seat; her mother sits in the back. They rarely speak in the car. At each stop, the daughter takes photos of her mother, who smiles, still seeming a little unused to being in front of the camera.

I think of a mother and son who stop me to ask if I will take their photo. I gladly agree. In return, they take several of me. Then we say goodbye.

A month later, I started at a new company.

One evening, while editing the videos from this trip, I saw myself from behind in a photograph. The camera pointed towards the sky. The colourful clouds were right behind me.
