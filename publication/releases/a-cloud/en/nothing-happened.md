# A Day When Nothing Happened

*Written in Chinese by River. Translated into English by ChatGPT.*

All we ever gain in this life is our experience of the world, our feelings, and our love.

In this sense, we can never “possess” money; we can only possess the feeling of being “rich.” I have wondered how I would spend the next day if someone suddenly gave me a hundred million. The answer is—I would spend it much as I do now. I would sleep in, get up at noon, make myself brunch, then do a little housework: laundry, tidying up. Next I would get my laptop, my notebook, and a pen, and put them in my bag. I would walk to the bus stop near my apartment and wait for the 199 to take me to the Lee Wee Nam Library at Nanyang Technological University. I would choose a comfortable seat—usually somewhere in a corner—and do whatever I felt like doing. I might learn something new, carry on with an ebook and copy out a few passages, or work on an essay, as I am doing now. There would also be times when I had tasks I simply had to finish.

I enjoy everything between waking and falling asleep. Perhaps, if I were wealthy, I would get a better bag or move to a larger apartment. But that would not change the rhythm or substance of my life.

So I think of myself as rich. My life now is much the same as it would be if I had a hundred million. And I have fulfilled my childhood dream of studying abroad. Through internships and part-time work, I saved enough for a few trips; before starting my first full-time job, I also have enough time to explore who I am. So I think of myself as “successful,” too.

To be honest, it is only now that I have truly felt happiness and contentment. I used to feel that there were always too many things to do, too many unknowns to worry about. I kept inventing a tomorrow that might “collapse at any moment”—and so I was afraid, anxious. I shut people out, resented the world, felt lost and helpless, and often seethed with indignation. I may have looked no different from anyone else—but the storms within a person are often known to that person alone.

I remember leaving my undergraduate university's library one day, two or three years ago. It was late autumn: empty streets, bare trees. Gusts of cold wind pushed up my sleeves. I tightened the scarf around my neck and walked on, shivering, feeling a sadness I could not quite account for. Perhaps I too had invented a rescuer for myself, like those who sink so low they cannot save themselves. Yet I also knew, with painful clarity, that growing up is a solitary journey no one else can make for you. Besides, “loneliness” is the human lot. For most of our lives, it follows us like a shadow.

One afternoon, I left my apartment to go to the library. The sunlight was bright, but it was not hot; there was a freshness I could not quite describe. I waited a long time for the lift, but it never came. So I stood waiting in the corridor. The breeze came in waves. Outside, branches danced and lush green leaves rustled. Sunlight fell on the little wooden table by my neighbour's door, its texture distinct, grain by grain. Suddenly I remembered a story:

> “A long, long time ago, a student who practised meditation went to see a wise man. The student began to discuss philosophical ideas, such as the nature of God, but the wise man said nothing. The student talked at length and asked many questions, yet the wise man remained silent. At last, exasperated, the student asked why he would not answer his questions. The wise man smiled and said softly, ‘I have been answering you all along. You simply have not been listening—God is stillness.’”<sup class="note-ref"><a id="ref-1" href="#note-1" role="doc-noteref" aria-label="Note 1">[1]</a></sup>

I do not know why that afternoon, ordinary as it was, touched me so deeply and stayed with me. In that moment I felt what God was teaching me—to live each moment with my whole being.

<div class="poem"><p>A day of doing nothing<br>
Gently, intently—I will linger over the sunset passing before me<br>
Feel light and warmth spread over my body, seep into every pore<br>
And in this moment, I raise my soul like a flag.</p></div>
